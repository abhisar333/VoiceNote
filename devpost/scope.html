VoiceNote

Scope · approved

Proof-of-concept scope — capture by talking, retrieve by reading.

The unique kernel

A note is an ordered list of segments, and a segment can be a recording. The recording stays attached to its position and replays in place, later, forever — with text as the skimmable companion rather than a replacement for it.

Delete that and VoiceNote is just another dictation app.

How it fits together

Who it's for

You — someone who gets an idea or remembers a task mid-flow, and won't stop what you're doing to type it out.

Today, two bad options: type it (slow, and it interrupts the thought) or voice-record it (fast in, painful out — sift through rambling every time, or hand-transcribe).

You don't sign, so sign language isn't the user's need.

Why it matters

"I was thinking about something else and I got the app idea. If I want to note it in a normal notes app I'd have to type it — that takes time and it interrupts my thought about what I was doing. If I use voice record I'd have to sift through long rambling to get different portions of the idea every time I need to review it, or type out a transcript."

Two frictions: capture without interrupting the thought, and retrieve without re-listening. Typing fails the first; a raw voice memo fails the second.

What "working" looks like

On a real Android phone: open the app, hold the mic, talk — and the transcript appears as a segment on its own. Later, the note reads as text; tapping a segment's words edits them, long-press replays the original audio in place. Force-quit and reopen — the note, its text, its order, and its audio all survive.

Demonstrable in well under a minute, which is what the demo video needs.

In · Later · Cut

  • One note; segments in order; typed segments.
  • Voice segments that record, auto-transcribe to text, keep the audio file, and replay in place.
  • Per-segment text you can edit, skip, or add after the fact.
  • Everything surviving an app restart.
  • The custom dev client auto-transcription requires — on Android this can be a local build (npx expo run:android), no EAS cloud queue.
  • Android only — built, run, and verified on Android. DESIGN.md said "iOS & Android"; the POC narrows to Android.
  • iOS support — DESIGN.md targeted "iOS & Android", but the POC is Android-only, so iOS moves here: unbuilt and untested, not assumed working. Reinstating it is mostly a build concern.
  • Sign segments (video record + in-place playback) — the same mechanism with a different recorder. Out of the POC because you don't sign, and it drags in expo-camera, a second recorder screen, camera permissions, and preview/retake.
  • Segment deletion, search, waveform/duration indicators, versioned migrations, cloud sync.
  • Automatic sign-language-to-text — no reliable model exists; the recording is kept, only auto-transcription is deferred.
  • Sync / cloud / accounts / collaboration; rich text, folders, tags, sharing/export.
  • Testing breadth and CI — a proof of concept, not a release.

Risks to prove

  • Concurrent microphone access — expo-av recording and expo-speech-recognition listening at once on the same mic. Unverifiable in Jest; must be checked on a real device — now an Android-only verification. Fallback: record first, transcribe from the finished file.
  • Build cost — the dev client is still the heaviest item in a 2–4 hour POC, but Android-only softens it: npx expo run:android builds locally, no cloud build or EAS account. Rebuild only when native config changes; JS changes just need npx expo start.
